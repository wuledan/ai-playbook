## Quick Verdict

On **August 21, 2026**, Kagi shipped an update that instantly became its most discussed feature of the year: a **setting that removes paywalled links from search results automatically**. The Hacker News thread hit **1,107 points and 362 comments** in under a day — a level of engagement Kagi rarely sees, and mostly positive.

The same release revamped the **Stocks widget** (now with ETF coverage and animated price charts) and upgraded **Kagi Assistant** with richer messages, cross-thread search, and configurable temporary-thread retention. It's a solid, workmanlike update on top of a service that has been quietly building for eight years.

**The bottom line:** Kagi remains the best answer to the question "is there a search engine that isn't advertising?" The new paywall filter is a genuinely useful control — but it lands in the middle of a real debate about whether hiding paywalled results helps readers or hurts journalism. At $10/mo for unlimited search, it's easy to recommend for heavy searchers; the $5 Starter tier is a teaser that most power users will outgrow.

## What Kagi Is (and How It's Different)

Kagi (Japanese for "key") is a **paid, ad-free search engine** founded by Vladimir Prelovac in 2018 and launched publicly in June 2019. It crossed **50,000 customers in June 2025** and has since kept compounding — the pricing page now positions itself explicitly against the ad-funded model:

> "Kagi has no ads, no tracking, no noise, and is fully funded by its users. We deliver fast, private, high-quality search. By joining, you're not just paying for a service; you're helping build a better web."

The structural difference matters: because users pay, Kagi doesn't need to harvest queries to sell ads. It doesn't run an ad network, doesn't sell data, and has no incentive to keep you clicking. That's the core value proposition, and everything else — lenses, bangs, redirects, the Assistant — is control surface layered on top.

## The August 21, 2026 Update, Feature by Feature

### 1. Paywalled-link removal (the headline)

A new setting under Kagi Search options lets you **strip paywalled links from results automatically**. Previously, users had to install a userscript, use the regex-redirect feature to rewrite paywalled domains to archive services, or rely on browser extensions like Bypass Paywalls Clean. Now it's a native toggle.

The HN reaction was immediate. Top comment `pelagicAustral`:

> "Killer feature. It would be awesome to have some plugin or userscript to auto-swap the e-begging scammy link for an Archive link instead."

`Nextgrid` pointed out the existing power-user path: "There is a regex replacement feature. You could build a list of known paywall domains to rewrite them to your desired paywall unblocker service." So the setting formalizes what advanced users were already doing manually — but now with zero setup.

### 2. Stocks widget revamp

The Stocks widget now covers **exchange-traded funds in addition to stocks**, and adds a **price chart with animated transitions between time windows** — the animation "instantly contextualizes how big the price fluctuations you're seeing are compared to the wider story," per the changelog. A small feature, but it fixes a real gap: previously the widget was just a ticker line with no chart context.

### 3. Kagi Assistant upgrades

Assistant, Kagi's AI chat layer (rolled out to all users in April 2025), got three improvements:

- **Richer messages** — user messages now render links, Markdown, and LaTeX (attributed to feedback from `@oxlvlnle` and `@EvacuatedTerminal`)
- **More powerful search** — search across all threads, sort by recency or alphabetically, and press `/` to filter by folder
- **Calmer settings** — choose whether temporary threads stick around for **24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days**, in a redesigned settings layout

None of this is revolutionary, but it's the kind of polish that makes a daily-use tool feel less like a toy.

| Starter | $5/mo | 300 searches/mo | Quick mode |

Annual billing gives 10% off. The Ultimate tier unlocks the flagship model roster: **GLM-5.1 (reasoning), Claude 4.8 Opus (reasoning), GPT 5.5, ChatGPT, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Gemini 3.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro (Preview), Mistral Medium 3.5, and Nemotron 3 Ultra**.

The honest read: **$10/mo Professional is the real product.** The 300-search Starter cap is roughly 10 searches a day — fine for casual use, tight for anyone who uses search as a work tool. Ultimate is worth it only if you actually use Assistant's Research mode heavily; the model gating is a real constraint.

## The Community Reception: 1,107 Points of Nuance

The HN thread split into three camps:

**Long-time users who've noticed the gap closing.** `roblh`: "The longer I use it, the more I like it. Initially I found myself going back to google fairly often, but unless I'm specifically trying to find a product for sale in my region, Kagi is almost always better now. Maybe Kagi got better, or google got worse, or probably both." `tempest_`: "I've been enjoying Kagi for the last couple years. Even as LLMs slurp up most of the internet and replace search I think Kagi is still useful."

**Power users trading setup tricks.** The thread became a mini-workshop on Kagi's redirect rules — `Phemist`: "I had Kagi configured to rewrite links old.reddit.com. Looks like I need to update it." Multiple users shared regex patterns for rewriting Reddit links to alternative front-ends after Reddit's logged-out access crackdown, and `teroshan` linked Kagi's redirects docs.

**The journalism counterargument.** The sharpest criticism came from `SamBam`: "Hmmm, so if you are looking for some news item, you'll only find AI-written clickbait articles covered in pop-up ads, rather than any news sites that employ journalists?" `demibabs` doubled down: "Yeah, I don't get it. Do people want actual journalism to go out of business?"

The rebuttal, from `ImPostingOnHN`: "I'd personally be okay with the paywalled article search results not showing up, since they are ads masquerading as usable search results." That's the crux: to many users, a paywalled result is a dead end dressed up as a result — to publishers, it's a discovery channel. Kagi's setting lets each user pick a side.

Kagi's moat is the control surface: no ad-funded engine will ship a native "remove paywalled links" toggle, because those links generate ad impressions. That's the clearest argument for paying.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Heavy searchers** (20+ queries/day) who want unlimited, ad-free, tracking-free search — $10/mo is trivial against the daily annoyance it removes
- **Privacy-conscious professionals** who run searches they'd rather not feed into an ad profile
- **Power users** who want lenses, bangs, and regex redirects — the most customizable index UI that exists
- **Journalism readers** who'd rather see fewer, higher-quality results than a paywall graveyard

## Who Should Skip

- **Casual searchers** (under 10 queries/day) — the free trial plus a private browser mode is probably enough
- **People who rely on Google for shopping/local discovery** — Kagi's product and map coverage is still thinner in many regions
- **Anyone who believes paywalled results should stay visible** — the new filter is on by default only if you turn it on, but the product philosophy clearly leans toward reader control over publisher reach

## FAQ

### Does removing paywalled links hide all news sites?

No — it removes links whose domains are detected as paywalled. Free news outlets, open-access publications, and preprint archives still appear. The setting is also off by default; you opt in.

### Is the 300-search Starter plan enough?

Roughly 10 searches/day. If you're a developer or researcher who searches throughout the day, plan on Professional. The upgrade path is instant and prorated.

### Which models does the Assistant use on each tier?

Starter and Professional get Quick mode with a default model set. Ultimate unlocks Research mode and flagship models including Claude 4.8 Opus (reasoning), GPT 5.5, Gemini 3.5 Pro, and DeepSeek V4 Pro.

### Can I replicate the paywall filter without paying?

Yes — browser extensions like Bypass Paywalls Clean or a userscript that rewrites paywalled domains to archive services achieve similar results. Kagi's version is just native, maintained, and one toggle.

### Is Kagi still independent?

Yes — Kagi Inc. remains user-funded and founder-led. It also makes Orion, its own browser, and runs a small ecosystem of services (Summarize, News, Assistant) around the core search product.

## Bottom Line

Kagi is the rare search product that gets *more* opinionated with age. The paywall filter is a small setting with large implications — it formalizes the "reader agency over publisher reach" stance and gives the 362-comment HN debate a concrete artifact to argue about. At $10/mo for unlimited, ad-free search with regex redirects, lenses, and a bundled Assistant, it remains the best-paid search subscription on the market. The $5 tier is a tease; the journalism trade-off is a judgment call; everything else is a clear win.
